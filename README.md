# Web3 Grant Application Template

The one-page format grant committees actually read. Used for applications to ecosystem foundations, protocol DAOs and VC grant programs. Fork it, fill it, send it.

**Why one page:** committees read hundreds of applications; clarity wins over polish. Every section below maps to a question a reviewer is silently asking.

→ Copy [`TEMPLATE.md`](TEMPLATE.md) and fill the blanks. Keep the whole thing under 600 words.

## The four things that get funded
1. **A specific thesis, not a category** — what exactly it does, for whom, better than what.
2. **A reason it's on-chain** — what the chain does that a server can't: custody, verification, composability.
3. **Evidence you can ship** — repo, prototype, past work. Links, not adjectives.
4. **A milestone the money completes** — amount, deliverable, date. "$40K → MVP on testnet in 8 weeks."

## What committees reject most often (2026)
The same five reasons come up across foundation, DAO and VC grant committees. Check your draft against each before sending.

1. **The ask is runway, not a deliverable.** "Extend our runway", "grow the community", "continue development" — nothing the reviewer can verify was completed. Name the artifact, the date and what "done" looks like.
2. **Nothing needs the chain.** The product would work the same on a normal server; the token or contract is decoration. If you can't say what breaks without the chain, expect a no.
3. **No proof the team can ship.** A deck and a roadmap, but no repo, no prototype, no shipped past work. Committees fund track record over promises; link something that runs.
4. **Budget doesn't match the scope.** A large ask for a few weeks of work, a tiny ask for a year of five engineers, or no line-item breakdown at all. Reviewers read the budget as a test of whether you understand the work.
5. **Wrong program, generic application.** Applying to a foundation for something outside its ecosystem, ignoring its published priorities or RFPs, or sending the same text to ten programs. Each program says what it funds; apply to that.

Not on the list but close behind: no maintenance plan after the grant, and closed-source deliverables sent to programs that require open source.
